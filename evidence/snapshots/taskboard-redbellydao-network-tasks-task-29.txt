Quant Overledger and Redbelly Interoperability Research

Research whether Redbelly could connect to Quant's Overledger, what that would take, and what it would offer institutions, in a fully sourced feasibility report.

Brief

On 24 September 2026 The Clearing House named Quant as the technology partner for its On-Chain Money Initiative, a tokenised deposit network for US banks expected in the first half of 2027. Overledger, Quant's interoperability platform, is becoming a route by which banks reach other chains. The community is already asking whether Redbelly could plug into it. This task answers that with research, not guesswork.

This is feasibility research only. Redbelly and Quant have no announced partnership or talks, and the report must not suggest otherwise. Redbelly has not published an official position on bridging, so treat anything not in a public source as unknown.

Scope of work
Explain what Overledger is and how chains connect to it today, from Quant's public documentation.
Assess whether Redbelly can connect now, and if not, what is missing.
Describe the technical work a connection would need, at a level a non engineer can follow.
Explain what a connection could offer institutions using Redbelly.
Explain how Receptor credentials and Tokeniser assets would behave across chains, and where compliance could break.
Say plainly where the answer is "not possible yet" or "unknown".
Write a short plain language summary the DAO can share with the community.
Deliverables
All are required
1.
The report, as a Google Doc, Word file or PDF. Length is your call, but it should be readable in one sitting.
2.
A short community summary (an X thread or a few paragraphs).
3.
A source list with a link for every factual claim.
Quality benchmarks
Every factual claim has a working source link.
Clear separation between what is confirmed, what is analysis and what is unknown.
A non technical reader can follow the main findings.
Neutral tone, neither hyping nor dismissing the idea.
Failure criteria
Any one fails the submission
Any wording that suggests a partnership, talks or plans between Redbelly and Quant.
Any factual claim without a source, or with a source that does not support it.
Technical claims about Redbelly or Overledger that are wrong.
Price talk, investment advice or promises of returns.
AI generated text submitted without checking, including made up facts or sources.
Personal credit inside the deliverable (names, handles, signatures, watermarks or "made by" lines). Contributors are credited by the DAO once the work is accepted, not inside the work itself.
