08Compliance

Receptor credentials and Tokeniser assets across chains

Analysis

How Receptor credentials would behave

A Receptor credential is a W3C verifiable credential held by the userS24. Its two methods travel differently. A Selective Disclosure proof is checked off-chainS26, so in principle any verifier could check it, including an externally hosted Overledger Flow AppS13; the app catalogue is not publicS14, so whether such an app exists is unknown. A Proof by Query check runs in a verifier contract against identity state published on RedbellyS8, so another chain cannot run the same check unless that setup is rebuilt there. The access credential unlocks write access on Redbelly, through a Redbelly network contractS5; no source shows another chain or Overledger reading it.

Analysis

How Tokeniser assets would behave

Tokeniser enforces a Tokeniser asset's rules on Redbelly at every transferS9S29. A lock-and-mint bridge, the pattern of the TASK-08 testnet bridgeS34, locks the asset on one chain and issues a copy on the other. In our reading, the copy then obeys the rules of the contracts and policies on the other chain. Tokeniser says its reach to other networks can be permissioned as a mandate allowsS9, but publishes no detail of how its transfer checks would apply to such a copy. Overledger's firewall decides access from its own list of users and their addressesS37, not from Receptor credentials.

Analysis

Where compliance could break

  1. A copy on another chain without eligibility checks could be passed to someone who never proved eligibility on RedbellyS9.
  2. Two lists of who is allowed: Receptor credentials on RedbellyS5 and Overledger's allowlistsS37. If they drift apart, one system allows what the other refuses.
  3. If a holder's credential stopped being valid on RedbellyS5, nothing documented would pass that change to a copy held elsewhere or to Overledger's allowlistsS37.
  4. Merged balancesS6 could mix a restricted asset with an unrestricted copy of the same asset.
  5. The list of tokens that may cross the Fusion bridge is set by Quant's governanceS37, not by the issuer.
  6. Tokeniser records every transfer on the issuer's register in real timeS30, and already keeps units pledged for a loan locked until it is repaidS31. Units locked in a bridge contract, or held on another chain, would need similar clear treatment in that register.
  7. Settlement timing changes: rollup withdrawals wait for a dispute windowS6, while Redbelly is final in one roundS9.
Analysis

The lowest risk pattern is for regulated assets to stay on Redbelly and for Overledger to carry only instructions and data at Level 1, so that every transfer still meets Redbelly's checksS3S5S9.
