07Institutions

What a connection could offer institutions

Analysis
  • One interface. An institution that already reaches other chains through OverledgerS12 could read Redbelly and send its own signed transactions through the same Gateway and firewall checksS13, without managing its own Redbelly RPC providers or node failoverS12, once a Redbelly node is registeredS3.
  • Rules stay on Redbelly. At Level 1 the Gateway passes each payload to a Redbelly node and passes back the native response unchangedS3, so Redbelly's access credential still applies to every sending accountS5, and Tokeniser's eligibility checks still apply to every transfer of a Tokeniser assetS9.
  • Key control. Institutions can keep and use their own keys in Overledger's modelS14.
  • Unified balances, only at Level 3. The Fusion Rollup can merge copies of the same token from several chains into one balanceS6. Redbelly is not among the documented connected networksS6, so as far as public sources show this is not available to Redbelly.
  • No known route to tokenised deposit networks yet. The Clearing House network will use Quant's technology for its interoperability layerS10, but the announcements do not say which chains it will use. Whether it will ever reach Redbelly, or any public chain, is unknownS10.
Analysis

Limits to weigh. Redbelly's developer portal already lists bridge routes through Celer, LayerZero and Lucid LabsS21S22S23, and Tokeniser points to EVM compatibility as its link to other networksS9. Overledger would add an institutional access layer rather than a first route out. Level 3 would also put Quant's governance signers in the path, since they control which tokens may cross the Fusion bridgeS37, and withdrawals from the rollup wait for a dispute windowS6, unlike Redbelly's single round settlementS9.
